Proposed Overall Research Objectives

Objective 1: Settlement Expansion and Its Impacts on Protected Areas

To use satellite imagery to monitor and map settlement expansion over time around and within protected areas.
To integrate satellite and drone imagery within a GIS framework to quantify settlement growth and identify areas of encroachment.
To assess the impacts of settlement expansion on habitat loss, habitat fragmentation, biodiversity, and human–wildlife conflict.

Objective 2: Hyperspectral Data and Species Richness

To use hyperspectral data to characterize differences in plant species, vegetation composition, and vegetation condition based on detailed spectral information.
To link hyperspectral information with field-based species richness measurements to identify spectral indicators associated with biodiversity.
To develop statistical and machine-learning models to determine the ability of hyperspectral variables to explain and predict species richness across landscapes.

Objective 3: Animal Population and Invasive Species Distribution

To integrate remotely sensed variables, including vegetation cover, habitat condition, and water availability, with animal observations and invasive species records.
To develop statistical and machine-learning models to identify factors influencing animal population abundance and invasive species distribution and to predict areas of high risk or suitable habitat.
To use model predictions to support wildlife monitoring, early detection of invasive species, and evidence-based conservation planning.

Objective 4: LiDAR-Based Forest Structure and Biomass Assessment

To use LiDAR scanners and drone-mounted laser scanners to generate accurate three-dimensional information on forest structure.
To measure individual tree height and develop detailed canopy-height models, integrating LiDAR data with field measurements to estimate above-ground biomass.
To apply the results to improve forest inventory, carbon assessment, biodiversity monitoring, and sustainable forest management.

Objective 5: Intelligent Road Mapping and Self-Driving Guide

To use satellite imagery, drone photographs, and existing park information to map and assess road networks within the national park and identify safe and efficient routes for different vehicle types.
To integrate the road network and other spatial information into an accurate digital navigation database.
To develop a self-driving guide that provides navigation, route directions, and location-based information to park visitors while improving visitor safety, tourism management, and protection of sensitive areas.
Database Development — Cross-Cutting Objective

Objective 6: Development of an Integrated Conservation GIS Database

To establish a centralized spatial database integrating satellite imagery, drone imagery, LiDAR data, hyperspectral data, field observations, wildlife records, invasive species records, settlement data, vegetation information, and road networks.
To develop standardized procedures for collecting, storing, updating, managing, and sharing geospatial and field-based conservation information.
To establish a database that supports spatial analysis, statistical and machine-learning modelling, monitoring, reporting, decision-making, and long-term conservation management.

In summary, the project would create an integrated GIS and remote-sensing system linking settlement dynamics, biodiversity, wildlife, invasive species, forest structure, biomass, and road networks into one database. This would provide a strong foundation for evidence-based management of the national park and development of future AI-based conservation and navigation systems.
